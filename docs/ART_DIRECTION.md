# Art direction

## Original workshop identity

The lab uses a Copper Observatory workshop: deep marine shade, cream surfaces, copper hardware, and teal accents. These are original project choices. Sparse geometry, deliberately placed silhouette breaks, broad shade shapes, and small pixel motifs make mechanisms easy to read.

## Research translated into practice

The supplied ABYSS X ZERO research supports texture-first modeling, Blender Closest sampling, atlas UVs, and authored vertex-color shading. Its creator examples distinguish matte material capability from dynamic-light contribution. These are technique references, not permission to reuse the game's art or claims about its complete implementation.

The supplied retro-game report motivates palette discipline, consistent perceived pixel density, restrained texture noise, and an explicit choice of era constraints. Its menu timings and controller behavior belong to game UI; the add-on uses Blender's normal accessible editor controls.

## Working rules

- Establish silhouette and camera readability before tiny surface detail.
- Use original small atlas motifs and hue-shifted shade ramps; sample pixel art with Closest.
- Use vertex color for broad local shade; inspect triangulation because interpolation follows triangles.
- Keep texture detail quieter than object silhouettes and the demonstrated mechanism.
- Show a clean baseline first. CRT, wobble, reduced resolution, dithering, or stepped motion are optional experiments with independent controls.
- Keep rendered typography and diagram text legible; the actual add-on controls use Blender's native UI.

Nearest sampling alone does not produce pixel art. Color management can alter output colors, and vertex colors can create intermediate shades. Color-count budgets describe source textures, not a universal rendered framebuffer limit. Numerical counts in this lab are original starter budgets, never measured budgets from the referenced games.

## Production boundary

Generated fixture meshes and textures are learning examples. They do not replace a project's artist or become approved production art through automation. Human approval covers visual identity, materials, silhouette, animation rhythm, and final export suitability.
